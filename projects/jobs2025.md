<h3>East Asia-related Job Market Data Report (2024–2025)</h3>
<p></p>
<em>Published January 20, 2026</em><br>
<p></p>

This page compiles information and visualizations on academic-oriented job postings related to East Asia during the 2024–2025 hiring season (from approximately June 2024 to July 2025). This year also marks five years since I began collecting East Asia-related job data, so reflections on the five-year trends for each section and/or subsection will also appear. For more information on how and why I began this project, please see my <a href="/projects/jobs2020/">first visualization page for the 2019–2020 academic job cycle</a>.
<p></p>
For the sake of convenience and to address any changes in my methods since last year,  I will reproduce my explanations on data collection from previous cycles below, and mark <b>new elements in bold and/or with a star ★</b>.
<p></p>
<b>To jump directly to the visualizations, click <a href="#dataviz">here</a></b>.
<p></p>
I hope that the information provided on this page will facilitate further dialogue on current and future developments in the East Asian Studies fields and will be used by departments and individuals to advocate for this area of study at their institutions. If you have any questions about the data or would like to request specific visualizations for departmental reports, grant requests, or other proposals (or have used my work for this purpose already), please do not hesitate to <a href="/contact/">contact me</a>.

<h4><b>About the Data: Caveats</b></h4>
<p></p>
As with previous reports, I begin by noting that <b>data is messy</b>. This is a reflection of the job ads themselves, which are even messier. A job ad might ask for something fairly specific, like a historian of contemporary China focusing on the environmental humanities, or simply list “East Asian Studies” with no additional information on a desired time period, specialization, or other qualifications. Because job ads are inconsistent in the terminology used to describe some academic positions, I have chosen not to do a vocabulary-based comparison on desired disciplinary expertise here. <b>However, if you are interested in exploring the key terms that appeared in job ads for the 2024–2025 market cycle, you can find a searchable table of them <a href="/projects/jobtable2025/">here</a></b>. For the purposes of producing this dataset, job ad language was taken at face value, and cannot account for any internal criteria that informed the scope of searches. How data is represented is also interpreted through the compiler. Others might have organized or categorized this data differently than I have.

<p></p>
Although I continue to increase the number of sources I use to locate job advertisements and solicit postings from colleagues outside of primarily English-speaking areas, <b>the data presented here is also reflective of both an English-language/North American bias and Japanese bias</b>. Many of the sites examined tend to focus on those positions, and job advertisements presented in other languages are only infrequently submitted to these venues, with the exception of Japan's centralized job posting site, JREC-IN. Some postings also may pass through informal means (such as mailing lists) only or through alternative lists to which I do not have access. Furthermore, some schools either do not or cannot afford to post their job openings through more widely-known channels.
<p></p>
This list is not exhaustive with regards to jobs that one might consider “alt-ac” or “academic adjacent,” as I focused to the best of my ability on those ads that readily circulated on academic job searching platforms.
<p></p>
<b>This year I incorporated job advertisements posted roughly between June 1, 2024 and July 31, 2025, along with the smattering of posts that went up until the publication of this report. This year I am able to do a five-year comparison, though data from the last 2-3 years should be considered the most accurate.</b> This is not only because of the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on the job market, but also because some job ad platforms were only later added, though I have done my best to include as much older data as was available when re-cleaning corpora from previous years.

  <h4><b>Overview & Continental Region</b></h4>
  <p></p>
    The data collected for the 2024–2025 academic job cycle totaled <b>1,058 job advertisements</b> globally. <b>This marks an decrease of 148 ads from the previous year.</b>The first year tracked, the 2020–2021 cycle, which has slightly less accurate data and also reflects the COVID-19 hiring freeze, had 677 job ads. Although this year's academic job market cycle began at roughly the same pace as the previous year, the sudden hiring freezes in early 2025 due to federal funding cuts in the United States likely stunted a significant number of hires, leading this year's decline.
  <p></p>
  &nbsp;<br>
  <center><img src="/images/01_All_Years.png" style="max-width:90%;"></center>
  <p></p>
  &nbsp;<br>
  During the 2024–2025 market cycle there were<b> 565 ads (53.4%) for job openings at Asia-based institutions</b>. Institutions in North America were the second highest employer, accounting for 365 ads (34.5%), whereas Europe offered only 113 positions (10.7%). There were 8 ads in Oceania, 2 ads in Africa, and 5 ads with no particular continental region specified (e.g. remote postdocs not tied to an institution).
  <p></p>
  &nbsp;<br>
  <center><img src="/images/02_Continental_Region_2425.png" style="max-width:90%;"></center>
  <p></p>
  &nbsp;<br>
  <font color="#0500b8">
  These trends are in keeping with the last market cycle, in which more job advertisements were released in Asia than North America (580 ads in Asia, vs. 467 ads in North America). On the whole, <b> over the last five years there has been an increase in the number of job advertisements in Asia, complemented by a decline in North America. </b> </font> Notably, the gap between the two continental regions has widened. Whereas in the 2023–2024 market cycle Asia-based advertisements accounted for 48% of all ads, and North America-based ads accounted for 39%, in the 2024–2025 market cycle Asia-based ads were 53% and North America-based ads were 34%. Europe remains a steady 11-12%.
  <p></p>
 <font color="#0500b8">Europe's job advertisement numbers have been on a seesaw trajectory, swinging from around 140 ads to around 115 ads year to year, on the downward turn this year. That said, Europe has generally accounted for between 10-11% of all ads globally for the last three years. The number of ads in Oceania (8 ads) and Africa (2 ads) were the same as last year, and more or less consistent with the last three market cycles.</font><p></p>
   <p></p>
  &nbsp;<br>
   <p></p>
  <center><img src="/images/03_Continental_20-25.png" style="max-width:100%;"></center>
  <p></p>
  &nbsp;<br>
  <p></p>
  There have been challenges for job seekers at either end of these five years of data collection, with the 2020–2021 job cycle impacted by the COVID-19 pandemic and the 2024–2025 federal hiring freezes in the U.S. halting many searches, which had implications well beyond North America. It is unclear at the present how these events will have an impact at a global scale for East Asian Studies.
  <p></p>
  &nbsp;<br>
  <em>Continental Regions: A Closer Look</em>
  <p></p>
  <center><img src="/images/04_Continental_Track_2425.png" style="max-width:100%;"></center>
    <p></p>
  &nbsp;<br>
  <p id="rcorners">
        <b><font size="4">During the 2024–2025 market cycle, Asia hosted over half of East Asian Studies hires worldwide. Asia also hosted 69% of all tenure-track or tenure-equivalent hires. 55% of jobs offered in North America were contingent, and only 30% were TT. In Europe, only 14% of ads were permanent positions, a roughly 50% drop since last year.</font></b>
  </p>
  <p></p>
  &nbsp;<br>
  If we consider hires by continental region for 2024–2025 by the track of the job advertised, we find that globally 429 job ads (40.5%) were for tenure-track (or tenure-track equivalent) positions and 535 job ads (50.6%) were for contingent (non-TT or postdoc) positions. There were 417 non-TT (39.4%) roles and 118 postdoc (11.2%) roles. The remaining 94 job ads (8.9%) were in the N/A category. The N/A category often accounts for positions that require East Asia/Asia-related expertise but fall outside the tenure system, such as administrative roles, some library or museum positions, etc.
  <p></p>
  <b>Asia hosted over half of East Asian Studies hires worldwide.</b>  Within Asia, 296 job ads (52.4%) were for tenure-track or TT-equivalent roles and 245 job ads (43.4%) were for contingent roles (221 non-TT, 24 postdocs). 24 ads were for N/A. 39.1% of contingent ads were for non-TT positions, and 4.2% were for postdocs. <b>Asia-based tenure track job ads make up 69% of all TT job ads in East Asian Studies globally.</b>
  <p></p>
  The data for Asia has two important caveats. First, jobs in Japan are more readily advertised through jRec-in, a widely-used central database for academic job advertisements, whereas Chinese and Korean institutions tend to release annual batch advertisements in their respective languages that are not widely circulated to Anglophone circles or hire candidates internally. This creates a significant imbalance in representation. Second, in the case of China, the widely advertised jobs are primarily for one-year contracts recruiting foreign language teachers, which somewhat skews contingent data.
  <p></p>
  <font color="#0500b8"><b>Since 2020, there has been an overall growth in the number of advertisements based in Asia.</b> Relatively permanent positions have not dipped below 40% of the jobs advertised there, with a boost from 48% to 52% in the last year (a five-year high). The percentage of non-TT job ads in Asia has hovered between 31% and 43% for the past five years, while postdoctoral roles have declined steadily from 16% in 2020 to roughly 4% this past market cycle.</font> Though Japan-based positions dominate this data, these are more positive trends than we find in other continental regions.
  <p></p>
  <b>In North America, during the 2024–2025 market cycle, only 110 job ads (30.1%) were for tenure track positions, whereas over half, 202 ads (55.4%), were for contingent roles.</b> 143 contingent ads (39.2%) were for non-TT positions (39.2%) and 59 contingent ads (16.2%) were for postdocs. This significantly higher number of postdocs is typical, as they are more often than not rooted in area studies centers that are more likely to exist in Anglophone contexts, rather than within Asia itself. For the N/A category, there were 53 ads (14.5%). <b>North America accounted for 37.8% of contingent job ads and 25.6% of tenure track job ads worldwide.</b>
  <p></p>
  <font color="#0500b8">From 2020 on, it appeared that the North American market was slowly recovering, with the largest number of overall advertisements during the 2022–2023 market cycle and tenure track positions on the rise over a four-year period since COVID. However, the number of advertisements in North America dipped slightly in 2023–2024 (from 515 ads to 467 ads) and then dropped more sharply this year (to 365 ads). <b>With the academic hiring freezes this year, there was an abrupt decline in the number of tenure track positions offered in North America</b>. Tenure track positions went from 21% of the total ads in the North American market cycle in 2020 to 27%, 30%, 35%, and then this year, 30% again. By contrast, taken as a percentage of the whole, non-TT roles went from 42% in 2020 and 2021 to 37%, then 31%, and rose to 39% this year.</font>
  <p></p>
  <b>For Europe</b>, there were 113 ads total, of which <b>only 16 ads were for permanent positions that would be considered roughly tenure-track equivalent</b>. By contrast, 80 jobs (70.8% of all EAS ads in Europe) were contingent, with 52 ads (46%) being non-TT and 28 ads (24.8%) being postdocs. There were 17 N/A ads (15%). Due to the lower number of overall jobs, these percentages can look more dramatic than those for Asia and North America, but the situation is no less distressing.
  <p></p>
  <font color="#0500b8"><b>Since 2020, there has been an overall decline in the number of permanent positions in Europe, and a steady decline in the percentage of permanent positions relative to contingent roles</b>. The decrease from 2023–2024 to 2024–2025 is particularly steep, going from 42 TT job ads last year to the 16 TT job ads this year. Permanent positions were 42% of offerings in Europe in 2020, 43% in 2021, but this year were only 14%. Contingent roles (non-TT and postdocs) were 51% of job ads in Europe in 2020, but are now 71%.</font>
  <p></p>
  <b>Oceania</b> on the whole has had very few job advertisements each market cycle. For 2024–2025 there were 8 job advertisements (4 in Australia and 4 in New Zealand), which is in keeping with last year's cycle (though last year 7 positions were in Australia and only 1 based in New Zealand). Of the job ads this year, 5 ads were tenure-track or TT-equivalent, 1 ad was non-TT, and 2 ads were postdocs. It is difficult to draw significant conclusions due to the overall lower number of positions available, but there were 7 TT advertisements in both the 2020–2021 and 2021–2022 market cycles.
  <p></p>
  This year saw 2 job advertisements based in <b>Africa</b> (both at the American University in Cairo) and both were TT/TT equivalent. There were also 2 job ads in Africa in 2022–2023 and 2023–2024, though they were split between Egypt and Morocco as well as between TT and non-TT. This year there were no jobs located in South America that I could locate. In 2023–2024 there was one TT role advertised in Brazil and 2 postdocs, one based in Chile and one based in Peru.

In <b>Asia</b>, 64 ads (32.2%) were TT or TT-equivalent vs. 135 ads (67.8%) non-TT. In <b>North America</b>, only 6 ads (7.4%) were TT while 75 ads (92.6%) were non-TT. In <b>Europe</b>, 1 ad was for a permanent position, while 21 were non-TT. <font color="#0500b8"><b>Over the last four years, the rate of contingency in Asia has fallen steadily about 10% each market cycle.</b> North America ranged between 94% and 95% contingent from 2020 to 2023, had an unusual 9% decline in contingent roles in 2023–2024, and now the percentage has climbed back to 92.6%. <b>North America (more specifically, the United States) has consistently had the worst record of reliance on contingent labor in the Language field over the last five years</b> (though it is admittedly difficult to make comparisons with, say, Europe, given its overall smaller number of ads).</font>

In Europe there was 1 advertisement for a permanent position in Chinese. Interestingly, in North America there were 3 TT ads for Chinese, 2 TT ads for Korean, and only 1 TT ad for Japanese. All of the Translation & Interpretation roles in Asia were for Chinese (3 ads), and North America had a spread of 1 ad each for Japanese and broad regional categories of East Asia/Asia OR the Middle East and East Asia/Asia OR South Asia.

Fewer overall jobs and fewer permanent jobs. In Asia the opposite was true, having an 11% increase in TT/TT-equivalent roles and an increase in the number advertised.</font>
  <p></p>
  Globally, of the top hiring school types, R1 institutions and SLAC/Bacc institutions are roughly equal in their poor record of tenure track hires, having 35% and 34% TT ads respectively. That said, given that R1 institutions host a significantly larger number of advertisements, they likely have a worse advertisement to hire record.
  <p></p>
</section>

<section id="pubpriv">
  <p></p>
  <h4><b>Public & Private Institutions</b></h4>
  <p></p>
  <p></p><center><iframe src="https://public.tableau.com/views/DataReport2025PubPrivFilter/PublicPrivateDash?:language=en-US&:sid=&:redirect=auth&:display_count=n&:origin=viz_share_link?:language=en-US&publish=yes&:display_count=n&:origin=viz_share_link&:showVizHome=no&:showShareOptions=false&:showShareOptions' value='false" width="100%" height="800" frameBorder="0"></iframe></center>
  <p></p>
  &nbsp;<br>
  <p></p>
  <p id="rcorners">
        <b><font size="4">This year public institutions hosted over half of all job opportunities (54%). Globally, 43% of public institution job ads were for tenure track roles, vs. 39% for private institutions. Asia's public and private institutions had an equal 52% rate of TT job ads, while North America's public institutions had a rate of 41% TT roles and private institutions merely 22% TT, a gap that widened over the last four years. For Europe, only 14% of ads at public institutions were permanent, and there was only 1 permanent position at private institutions this year.</font></b>
  </p>
  <p></p>
  &nbsp;<br>
  <b>During the 2024–2025 market cycle public institutions comprised 572 ads (54.1%) of the total 1,058 job advertisements, with private institutions accounting for 472 (44.6%), and another 14 ads in the N/A category.</b> On a global level, the raw number of job ads for each institution type has varied, <font color="#0500b8">but by relative percentage there has been <b>a decline in public institutions</b>, from 64.1% in 2020–2021 to 62% in both the following years, then 58.3% last year, and finally 54.1% this year.</font> 
  <p></p>
  The ratio of public and private institutions (as well as their level of financial support and size) differs significantly by continental region (and in some cases location), but some broad information and comparisons will still be provided here. For <b>Asia</b>, there were 304 ads (53.8%) for public institutions, 260 ads for private (46%), and 1 ad for N/A (0.2%). <font color="#0500b8">Though public has remained more prevalent in Asia over the last five years, the gap between private and public has narrowed, with more private institutions posting ads. In <b>North America</b>, it was the opposite, with more job ads through private institutions than public for the last five years, but the gap was narrower for the last three job market cycles and grew wider this year.</font> For 2024–2025, there were 206 ads for private (56.4%), 156 ads for public (42.7%), and 3 ads (0.8%) for N/A. In <b>Europe</b>, institutions are overwhelmingly public, so with 104 ads (92%) in public, only 4 ads (3.5%) in private, and 5 ads (4.4%) for N/A, the numbers are disparate; <font color="#0500b8">this immense difference is consistent across the last five years, all above 92% public institution ads.</font>
  <p></p>
  From a global perspective, <b>42.7% of public institution job ads were for TT/TT-equivalent positions</b>, while 46.3% were contingent (36.2% non-TT, 10.1% postdoc), and 11% were N/A. <b>For private institutions, TT roles accounted for 39.2%</b>, contingent for 54.9% (44.3% non-TT, 10.6% postdoc), and N/A was 5.9%. Within <b>Asia</b>, public institutions had a higher percentage for TT job ads than the global rate, at 52.6% TT and 40.5% contingent (33.9% non-TT, 6.6% postdoc), and 6.9% N/A. Their private institutions were also higher than global numbers, at 52.3% TT, 46.5% contingent, and 1.2% N/A. Notably, <b>there was no significant difference in percentages of permanent roles between public and private institutions in Asia</b>, <font color="#0500b8">and there was greater parity than last year</font>. For <b>North America</b>, the percentage of TT job ads at public institutions was just under the global percentage, having 41% TT and 42.9% contingent (33.3% non-TT, 9.6% postdoc), and 16% N/A. However, the <b>private institutions were much lower</b>, at 22.3% TT job ads, 63.6% contingent (44.2% non-TT, 21.4%), and 12.1% N/A. <b>Private institutions in North America thus offered roughly half the number of tenure track roles as public institutions did</b>. <font color="#0500b8">The gap between the two in terms of TT offerings has gradually widened from near equivalence four years prior.</font> In the case of <b>Europe</b>, despite the overwhelming prevalence of public institutions for job advertisements, only 14.4% of those ads were for permanent roles, while 69.2% were contingent (49% non-TT, 20.2% postdoc) and 16.3% were N/A. Private institutions had only 4 jobs ads, one of which was permanent. 
  <p></p>

  <h4><b>Top Hiring Institutions</b></h4>
  <p></p>
  The following charts show the top 10-15 institutions (globally, and by continental region) that hosted the most hires in East Asian Studies this year. <b>The institution with the greatest number of job ads worldwide was Harvard University (29 ads), followed by Stanford University (18 ads), University of Hong Kong (16 ads), Tohoku University (14 ads), and Chinese University of Hong Kong (13 ads).</b> Hiroshima University and Waseda University each had 12 ads, and Columbia University, Hongkik University, Paris Cité University, and University of Oxford each had 10 ads. The top 11 hiring institutions were thus based in the United States (3 schools), Japan (3 schools), Hong Kong (2 schools), the United Kingdom (1 ad), South Korea (1 ad), and France (1 ad). <b>Of the 11 institutions represented, the top 2 were private, though only 5 total were private</b>. With the exception of Hongkik University, all were R1 very high research institutions. 
  <p></p>
  &nbsp;<br>
  <center><img src="/images/10_GlobalTopHire_2425.png" style="max-width:80%;"></center>
  <p></p>
  <b>The top eleven global hiring institutions for the 2024–2025 cycle combined had 37.7% tenure track/TT-equivalent positions and 53.9% contingent job ads (35.1% non-TT, 18.8% postdocs), with 8.4% N/A.</b> The six institutions in Asia had a combined 63.6% TT vs. 32.5% contingent job ads (26% non-TT, 6.5%) postdocs, <b>a stark contrast to the three institutions in North America, which only had 12.3% tenure track job ads vs. 73.7% contingent (31.6% non-TT, 42.1% postdoc)</b>, and Europe, with its two institutions having an 10% TT vs. 80% (all non-TT) roles. 
  <p></p>
  <font color="#0500b8"><b>Harvard has had the highest number of job advertisements for the past five years</b>. Last year, Harvard, Waseda and the Chinese University of Hong Kong were the top three hiring institutions, though there were 16 institutions that released 10 or more job ads. Looking at the number of job advertisements in East Asian Studies for the top five hiring institutions over the last five years, Harvard had a combined 130 job advertisements, followed University of Hong Kong with 88 job advertisements, Chinese University of Hong Kong with 79 advertisements, Stanford University with 66 job advertisements, and the University of Oxford with 55 job advertisements.</font>
  <p></p>
  <font color="#0500b8"><b>Of the 130 job ads out of Harvard over the last five years, 7 ads were for tenure track positions</b>, 70 ads were for postdocs, 22 ads were non-TT, and 31 ads were for N/A. At University of Hong Kong (88 ads total), there were an equal number of TT and postdoc ads (29 each), 27 non-TT, and 3 N/A. At Chinese University of Hong Kong, of the 79 advertisements released 45 ads were TT, 28 were non-TT, 5 were postdocs, and 1 was N/A. For Stanford, of 66 job advertisements total, of which 7 ads were TT, 35 were postdocs, 9 were non-TT, and 15 were N/A. Finally, the University of Oxford had 55 job ads, of which 9 were TT, 22 were non-TT, 15 were postdocs, and 9 were N/A. <b>As the numbers above show, although Harvard was the top hiring institution around the globe, only about 5% of those advertisements were for tenure track jobs, whereas the University of Hong Kong, in second, had about 33% tenure track advertisements</b>.</font>

The top ten hiring institutions were the University of Hong Kong (16 ads), Tohoku University (14 ads), Chinese University of Hong Kong (13 ads), Hiroshima University (12 ads), Waseda University (12 ads) Hongik University (10), Tokyo International University (8 ads), Nagoya University (7 ads), Osaka University (7 ads), and Toyo University (7 ads). <b>Six of the ten institutions were public, including the top four</b>. Though the top three institutions were R1, the rest were a mix of D/PU and R2 institutions.

The top hiring institution last year was Waseda University, followed by Chinese University of Hong Kong and University of Hong Kong. The latter two have been in the top three for four years.</font>
  <p></p>
  Collectively, <b>the 2024–2025 Asia-based institutions had a rate of 55.7% tenure track/TT-equivalent job ads</b>, a 41.5% contingency rate (36.8% non-TT, 4.7% postdoc), and 2.8% N/A. At the University of Hong Kong, which had the highest number of job ads, 5 ads out of the 16 ads total were tenure track. At Tohoku University, with 14 ads total, there were 10 TT job ads, and the same number at Chinese University of Hong Kong, with 13 ads total.

In second was Stanford University (18 ads), then Columbia University (10 ads), the Defense Language Institute Foreign Language Center (9 ads), University of Chicago (8 ads), University of Pittsburgh (8 ads), Princeton University (7 ads),University of Pennsylvania (7 ads), and Yale University (6 ads). Only two schools out of nine top hiring institutions were be public; the top three hiring schools were all private. With the exception of the Defense Language Institute, all the top hiring schools were R1 very high research institutions. Note they are all based in the United States.

Harvard, Princeton, and Stanford were the top three hiring institutions in the previous two years, and Harvard, Stanford, and Columbia were in the top three the year before that.</font>

Compare with Asia’s top hiring institutions, which had 55.7% tenure track and 41.5% contingency. At Harvard, which had the highest number of job ads (29 ads), 2 job ads were for tenure track positions, with 14 ads for postdocs, 6 ads for non-TT, and 7 ads for N/A. Stanford, in second with 18 ads, had only 1 TT position, 9 postdocs, and 8 non-TT ads. Columbia, with 10 ads total, had a better record with 4 ads for TT jobs, 1 postdoc, 4 non-TT, and 1 N/A.

  <p></p>
  <h4><b>A Glimpse into Digital Labor</b></h4>
  <p></p>
  The majority of digital labor that goes into research projects and public facing scholarship is invisible, with the many hours spent crunching numbers, coding, or designing user interfaces or graphics unknown to the casual observer. For this reason I like to include descriptions of the labor that goes into this job data collection at the end of each report. Being transparent about our labor is crucial to not only educating others on how these projects are undertaken but also to facilitating a more equitable academia by freely providing that information.
  <p></p>
  I have been collecting this job data for the last five years. In 2022, it was supported by an <a href="https://www.asianstudies.org/grants-awards/striving-for-diversity-equity-and-inclusion-in-asian-studies-humanities-grants-for-asian-studies-scholars/">Association for Asian Studies and National Endowment for the Humanities Striving for Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion in Asian Studies Digital Humanities grant</a> for one year. Nevertheless, this has meant four years of labor funded only through generous sponsorship by <b><a href="https://www.patreon.com/prcurtis">Patreon</a></b> <a href="https://www.patreon.com/prcurtis"><img src="/images/patreon_circle1.png" alt="Patreon" width="25px"></a> patrons.
  <p></p>
  This year in particular I feel I have much improved my data collection and curation, so I spent a significant amount of time—far more than in previous years—working and reworking my materials to effectively review and (re)clean the previous years’ data. This process allows me to be more consistent in my analyses, particularly multi year comparisons.
  <p></p>
  Over the course of the last five years I have searched for new postings on a weekly basis across over a dozen+ websites. I record them in my annual data file, clean up the data, select keywords from the advertisement to feature in the filter table, and then post a brief summary of the latest ads on social media. I also write a brief data report on my thoughts about trends in progress for patrons on my <a href="https://www.patreon.com/prcurtis">Patreon</a>. This work is typically done on my Fridays and over the weekend.
  <p></p>
  I use the tool Timeular (now known as "Early") to track the hours I spend on my digital projects and other professional activities, particularly those for which I am (or am not) paid. To provide a rough estimate of how much time has been spent on my job data alone over the last year, I provide visualizations from my time tracking below.
